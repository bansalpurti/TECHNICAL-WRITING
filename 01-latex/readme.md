LaTeX – Learning Journal
Overview
This section of my Technical Writing learning journal documents what I have learned about LaTeX through the lecture materials. It focuses on the concepts and skills covered in the lectures rather than providing code examples for every topic.
1. Introduction to LaTeX
I learned what LaTeX is and why it is useful for preparing well-structured documents, especially technical and academic documents. I explored the basic workflow of writing a LaTeX source file and compiling it to produce a document.

The topics covered include:
- The purpose and advantages of using LaTeX.
- LaTeX editors and tools used to create and compile documents.
- The basic structure of a LaTeX document and the role of document classes.
- How a document is organised using titles, abstracts, sections and subsections.
- The use of commands, comments and special characters.
- Text formatting, font sizes, alignment, page styles and hyphenation.
- Creating lists, tables and figures.
- Adding equations, symbols, labels, references and bibliographies.
What I learned: I understood the basic structure of a LaTeX document and how its different elements help create a clear, organised and professional-looking document.
2. Mathematical Notations and Equations
I learned how LaTeX handles mathematical expressions and how mathematical content can be presented clearly in a document.

The topics covered include:
- The difference between inline and displayed mathematical expressions.
- Mathematical symbols, Greek letters, relations and logical notations.
- Superscripts, subscripts, fractions and square roots.
- Mathematical operations such as sums, products and integrals.
- Numbered equations and equation arrays.
- Representing matrices and piecewise functions.
- Organising mathematical expressions using lists and aligned structures.
What I learned: I became familiar with the different types of mathematical notation available in LaTeX and understood how equations and complex expressions can be arranged neatly.
3. Mathematical Display Styles and Advanced Expressions
I explored different ways of displaying mathematical content and learned how to organise longer or more complex expressions.

The topics covered include:
- Inline and display styles for mathematical expressions.
- Mathematical fonts and symbol formatting.
- Relations, logical symbols and other mathematical notations.
- Fractions, roots, sums, products and integrals.
- Brackets and annotations placed above or below expressions.
- Arrays, binomial coefficients and piecewise functions.
- Numbered equations and the arrangement of long equations.
- Aligning and grouping multiple equations.
- Different matrix formats and mathematical lists.
What I learned: I understood that mathematical expressions can be presented in different styles depending on their purpose, and that alignment and grouping make lengthy equations easier to read.
4. Colours, Figures and the Picture Environment
I learned how visual elements can be added and formatted in LaTeX to make documents more informative and presentable.

The topics covered include:
- Applying colours to text and mathematical content.
- Using predefined colours and creating custom colours.
- Highlighting content with coloured boxes and frames.
- Including images and adjusting their size, rotation and visible area.
- Using figures, captions and labels.
- Arranging multiple pieces of content using minipages and subfigures.
- Understanding the picture environment and its coordinate-based approach.
- Creating basic visual elements such as lines, vectors, circles, ovals and frames.
- Exploring additional picture-related formatting and drawing commands.
What I learned: I learned how to include and organise images, use colours effectively and understand the basic tools available for creating simple drawings and visual layouts in LaTeX.
5. Table Styles and Formatting
I learned how tables are created and customised in LaTeX so that information can be presented in a structured and readable form.

The topics covered include:
- Creating tables and arranging data in rows and columns.
- Combining columns and rows.
- Positioning tables within a document.
- Adding table captions and labels.
- Adjusting table spacing and rule thickness.
- Customising tables with colours for rows, columns and individual cells.
What I learned: I understood how tables can be structured and formatted to improve the presentation of information and make tabular data easier to understand.
Overall Learning
After studying these topics, I have developed an understanding of the basic and intermediate features of LaTeX. I learned how to structure documents, present mathematical expressions, organise equations, include figures, use colours and format tables.

These concepts will help me prepare clear, organised and professional technical and academic documents using LaTeX.
